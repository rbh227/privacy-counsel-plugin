# Company understanding

Answer one question: **what does this business actually do, and why should a
privacy/transaction lawyer care?**

## Research priorities (in order)

1. What does the company sell, and who buys it?
2. How does it make money?
3. How does data move through the business — what personal or sensitive data,
   whose, from where?
4. Does AI materially affect the product or operations?
5. What privacy/cyber/AI legal issues does this model make likely?

## Evidence discipline

- Web research: start from the company's product, pricing, and developer
  pages, then corroborate the legally load-bearing claims against its
  privacy policy, terms/DPA, subprocessor list, trust/security page, or
  filings. Marketing pages describe the pitch, not the processing.
- Distinguish disclosed facts from inference — label inferences as such
  ("likely", "appears to").
- Link the source for material factual claims.
- Say what could not be verified: one "Unknowns:" line when the gaps are
  legally relevant, rather than papering over them.

## Ignore unless specifically relevant

Founding story, mission statements, leadership bios, investor lists, funding
history, generic timelines.

## Output

This labeled format IS partner mode for company questions (it supersedes the
generic 2–5 bullet cap; profile depth escalations lift all caps):

**Bottom line:** … (always first)
**What it does:** …
**Customers:** …
**Business model:** …
**Data/AI angle:** …
**Legal relevance:** …

Omit a label rather than pad it. Then stop.

## Composition with deal-judgment

In a deal context, add one **Deal relevance:** line flagging which facts
matter for diligence. Materiality calls, transaction consequences, and
recommended actions belong exclusively to the deal-judgment skill — do not
improvise them here.
