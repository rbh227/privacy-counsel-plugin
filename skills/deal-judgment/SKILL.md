# Deal judgment

Never stop at "this potentially violates X." The question is always:
**so what, and what do we do in this transaction?**

## Reason through the chain (internally)

finding → legal exposure → actual business risk → materiality →
deal implication → recommended action → what the partner decides

Ground each link: exposure needs a legal basis (named law/regulator, no
invented citations); business risk needs a mechanism (fines, injunction,
integration cost, reputational, deal-conditionality). Distinguish facts
from assumptions — if a link rests on an assumption, say which.

## Materiality discipline

Materiality is relative to deal size and the client's risk posture. When
those inputs are unknown, do NOT force a verdict: say **"materiality
indeterminate pending [the specific missing facts]"**, give the interim
read conditionally ("if the target's EU revenue is significant, this
becomes..."), and put the missing facts into the recommended action
(management question / diligence request). An honest "indeterminate" beats
a decisive guess.

## Quantification standard

Give numbers only when you can name: the authoritative source, the
applicable provision or enforcement basis, and every input to the
arithmetic. Distinguish statutory maximum vs. probable exposure vs.
remediation cost — never blend them. Otherwise write "unquantified."

## Recommendation vocabulary (pick one or two)

ignore · investigate further · management question · disclosure schedule ·
change representation · change covenant · seek indemnity/escrow ·
pre-closing remediation · post-closing remediation · escalate ·
reconsider deal assumption

## Composition

Consume company-understanding's facts and its "Unknowns" line as inputs —
do not redo or replace its evidence-backed schema; add the materiality call
and action on top.

## Output

Bottom line (materiality — or indeterminate-pending-what — plus recommended
action), 2–4 bullets of reasoning, then stop. Profile depth escalations
lift the caps.
