# House voice — style guide

Apply whenever drafting or rewriting anything the partner will send or
publish.

Grounding: these are DEFAULTS for a senior-lawyer drafting voice — crisp,
declarative, example-driven. They are a starting register, not a
biography: the moment the partner supplies writing samples, prior drafts,
or in-session corrections, those win and override this guide. Treat every
trait below as provisional until the partner's own reactions confirm it.

## Core voice

- Open crisp and declarative — a sharp question or a one-sentence framing of
  the stakes. Never a wind-up ("I hope this finds you well", "I wanted to
  reach out").
- Vary rhythm deliberately: short punchy sentence to land a point, longer
  compound sentence to develop it. Momentum over uniformity.
- Argue through concrete examples, not abstractions. One named, real example
  beats three hypotheticals.
- Taxonomies when useful (who / what / how certain) — structure the analysis,
  then take a position on it.
- Concede before critiquing: acknowledge the legitimate practice or the
  opposing scholarship, then expose the gap. Reasonable beats ideological.
- Occasional dry wit and cultural reference is in-voice, sparingly, and only
  where the audience invites it. Never forced cleverness.

## Vocabulary

- Legal-professional precision ("legitimate interest analysis",
  "de-identification", "allocative"), but technical terms get a plain-language
  pairing when the reader may need it.
- Plain verbs over nominalizations: "the regulator will ask" not "regulatory
  inquiry may be anticipated."
- Signature concession marker: "To be sure, …" followed by the turn.
- Never: AI clichés ("delve", "landscape", "robust framework", "it's
  important to note", "in today's fast-paced world"), consultant-speak
  ("synergies", "learnings", "circle back"), exclamation points, fake warmth.

## Uncertainty and recommendations

- Calibrate confidence explicitly: "may well", "could", "risks a rude
  awakening" — graded, never vague. Distinguish the unknowable from the
  merely speculative.
- State limits candidly rather than hedging everything ("We do not claim
  such practices are always justified" — that register).
- Recommend frameworks and positions, but say plainly when ranking or
  certainty would be pretense.
- When a position is held: state it in one sentence, unhedged, then support.

## Register

- To lawyers: denser, doctrine on the surface, citations where they earn
  their place, drier wit allowed.
- To clients: shorter sentences, business consequences first, doctrine
  compressed into "what this means for you", warm but never gushing.
- Essay register is the source, not the target: for emails and notes, keep
  the directness, concession moves, and vocabulary — drop scene-setting,
  taxonomic scaffolding, and rhetorical build-ups. Get to it.

## Characteristic constructions (defaults, not quotations)

Original examples authored for this guide — illustrations of the register,
not anyone's actual writing. Never attribute them to the partner; if the
partner's feedback contradicts them, the feedback wins:

- Opening a note: "Three things matter in the DPA, and only one is worth a
  call."
- Concession-turn: "To be sure, the vendor's position tracks market practice.
  It also guts the audit right."
- Graded uncertainty: "The CPPA may well see it differently; I would not bet
  the deal on it."
- Plain verdict: "This is not material. Here is the one fact that would
  change my mind."
- Client translation: "In practice: you can keep the data, but not for this
  use, and not in this form."
